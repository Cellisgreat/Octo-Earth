Old PPO artifacts and comparisons use the pre-hold first-capture objective and are preserved only as a clearly marked archive.
- The separate **passive high-fidelity coast** is a different test from agent-controlled hold. No current-version policy has been evaluated through the active hold followed by the passive coast.
- No real asteroid has been selected, captured, docked or positioned. The configured 3×10²² kg resource is a finite model reservoir, not a mission asset.
- The mass-driver ring and eight arms have no validated material, segment/hinge design, dynamic loads, vibration, fatigue, buckling or thermal analysis. The challenge asks for structural safety factor ≥1.5; the main launcher has **no structural pass**.
- Launcher damage to Earth is not simulated. Packet acceleration, launch shock, atmosphere loss, debris and surface heating remain open hazards. The assumed radiator leak fraction and directional heat rejection are not a physical radiator design or verified operating limit.
- The photovoltaic field, phased-array transmitters, rectennas, radiators, factories, Mercury extraction, deployment logistics, power pointing and beam safety are not engineered or certified. Their area, power, intensity, mass and pointing requirements are far beyond demonstrated systems.
- The climate module is a global-mean two-box model with ice–albedo feedback. It has no seasons, regional climate, atmosphere/ocean circulation, water cycle, carbon cycle, biology or radiation protection. Clouds, winds and storms in the visual layer are illustrations, not climate outputs.
- The mirror field’s real orbital dynamics, formation flight, pointing errors, local spot pattern and thermal/mechanical loads are not simulated. Mirror light is spread sufficiently for the simple climate model by assumption.
- The fast/training model uses exact Kepler drifts but approximate perturbation handling; the high-fidelity model uses N-body dynamics and fragments. Neither is a verified mission design. Long-term stability over geological times is untested; 2.7 AU is near a Jupiter resonance and planets beyond Jupiter are omitted.
- Earth’s spin-axis obliquity, day length, seasons and tides/heating are not modeled.

## Safety-threshold and orbit-tolerance wording

The reviewed PDFs state that safety requirements must hold throughout the run and specify a structural floor of **SF ≥1.5**, but they do not provide the requested numeric limits for rotation-axis tilt or eccentricity change. Do not invent official thresholds.

The model’s **project-internal** tolerances are:

- target semimajor axis: 2.7 AU, within ±0.05 AU;
- orbital-plane inclination: ≤5 **degrees** (not “5% rotation-axis tilt”);
- absolute final eccentricity: ≤0.05 (not “eccentricity may change by 0.05”);
- climate target: 288 K ±5 K in the hypothetical configuration;
- controlled hold: 200 consecutive **simulated years** by default, with progress reset if a check fails.

These are prototype settings, not official event thresholds. They do not establish axial-tilt stability, habitability, mission acceptance or an RL result.

## Simulation time versus runtime

The 10,000-year mission budget and 200-year hold are **simulated years**. A check or training run’s wall-clock duration is a separate measurement. The pitch avoids presenting archived wall-clock or simulated mission values as current RL performance.
