This page maps the supplied rules, physics estimates, ten validation benchmarks, enablers and presentation criteria to the OCTO-EARTH prototype. The main model moves Earth only through reaction-mass ejections; the agent never edits Earth’s velocity directly. Passing a calculation benchmark is not the same as validating every hardware design or winning condition.

Important rulebook caveat: the participant guide requires the rulebook’s safety thresholds to hold for the entire run, but the supplied PDFs do not include those threshold values. They are not invented here. The guide does explicitly require every structural safety factor to remain at or above 1.5; this project does not certify its launcher or reflector structure to that requirement.

1 · Challenge acceptance criteria

Reach the orbit

≤ 10,000 simulated years

Earth must reach the target orbit. Keep simulated mission time separate from wall-clock runtime.

Stay safe throughout

whole run not just the endpoint

Every applicable safety threshold must hold throughout; supplied PDFs do not expose the rulebook thresholds.

Structure survives

SF ≥ 1.5 every structural element

Check every segment and hinge, not only total force or an average arm load.

Validated, conservative physics

Every applicable benchmark passes. Book every force with its reaction partner; conserve energy and momentum. A force internal to Earth/its structure cannot move Earth’s centre of mass.

No unresolved flags

Only unresolved flags that arise from explicitly declared hypothetical enablers are allowed. Do not hide infeasibility, climate failure, overload or missing physics behind an animation.

How the current RL episode handles arrival: it no longer ends at the first in-window state. The agent stays in control and must keep the orbit and climate within their configured tolerances for validate_years (default 200 simulated years); leaving tolerance resets that hold clock. This project-specific hold is inside the 10,000-year episode budget and is not an additional rulebook threshold. High-fidelity evaluation then runs a separate validate_years zero-ejection coast as a passive stability test; that coast is not PPO training feedback. A current-version PPO evaluation has not yet been run, so this implemented criterion is not a success claim.
Project orbit tolerances versus requested tilt: the hypothetical preset uses target semimajor axis 2.7 AU (±0.05 AU), orbital-plane inclination ≤5° (degrees, not 5% and not Earth’s spin-axis/obliquity), and absolute final eccentricity ≤0.05 (not a limit on how much eccentricity may change). These are project-internal tolerances only. The reviewed PDFs do not specify numeric rotation-axis-tilt or eccentricity-change thresholds. Earth’s axial tilt, spin, seasons and related habitability effects are not simulated.

3 · Physics that must be represented honestly

Gravity, bodies and numerical method

  • At minimum model the Sun, Earth and Moon; add planets when feasible. This project includes Mars, Mercury, Venus and Jupiter; planets beyond Jupiter are omitted.
  • Orbit / field calculations use 64-bit numbers. The high-fidelity model uses N-body gravity and a fourth-order Yoshida symplectic integrator; timestep-halving convergence and no-push energy checks are automated.
  • The fast training model uses exact hierarchical Kepler propagation and approximate planetary kicks; the Sun’s tide on the Moon is omitted in that model. The high-fidelity model includes it.
  • Encke-style deviation integration is used for the collector-belt station-keeping study around an exact reference orbit; do not interpret that as Earth’s primary orbit integrator.
  • Fast vibration, circuit and thermal response require their own shorter physical time step. The current project does not solve those fast hardware dynamics.

Momentum and the Lorentz force

  • For a conductor, integrate dF = I dl × B; a scalar ILB is only a magnitude estimate.
  • Current needs a closed return path. A closed loop in a uniform field has zero net force. Equal 45° arms also cancel in a uniform field.
  • State where the reaction lands: Earth’s core, another arm or the ring is internal; external plasma, photons, an asteroid, or escaping ejecta can transfer external momentum if the partner is modeled.
  • The Sun’s field or Earth’s magnetic field is not a reactionless wall. Identify and model the source/current/plasma carrying the momentum.

Habitability is a model output, not a promise

At 2.7 AU the guide’s simple equilibrium estimate is about 155 K. The project’s two-box climate model has a calibrated warm branch and an ice–albedo feedback; its no-mirror snowball result is about 152 K. Those values differ because the approximations differ. The project shows temperature, ice line and a growing-area indicator, but it is not a full atmosphere/ocean/biology model and does not certify liquid water, air, radiation shielding or a safe Moon for every possible trajectory.

The blackbody benchmark (254 K at 1 AU) is intentionally separate from the project climate model’s present-day 288 K calibration, which represents greenhouse warming.

4 · Structural loads and safety factor

Required definition: SF = allowable stress / actual stress; the guide requires SF ≥ 1.5. For a member, screen combined normal stress with σ = N/A ± Mc/I and Euler buckling with Pcr = π²EI/L².

Check each segment and hinge for axial and bending load, Euler buckling, self-weight, gravity variation with altitude, rotation, Coriolis effects, moving-joint inertia, Lorentz loads, heat and vibration. A 12,000 km arm is not a small attachment; its length exceeds Earth’s radius, gravity changes along it, and its natural vibration can be very slow.

What this build does

  • Runs the guide’s cantilever self-weight formula check and exposes general axial/bending/Euler screening helpers in octoearth_rl.py.
  • The current p40 model 1 shows a transparent assumed allowable arm load, its load ratio and the implied proxy SF = 1 / load_ratio.
  • Warns when that proxy drops below 1.5 (about 36.7% beam power under this model’s proportional-load assumption).
  • Keeps the later break cascade only as a deterministic visual demonstration.

What remains unverified

  • No real arm material, segment cross-section, hinge geometry, connection strength, motor, fatigue curve or allowable stress is specified.
  • The main launcher’s packet acceleration, segment/hinge load distribution, buckling, self-weight, rotation and vibration are not coupled to the simulation.
  • The p40 proxy is not an engineering safety factor and does not satisfy or certify the challenge’s SF ≥ 1.5 condition.
  • The PDFs refer to a separate rulebook appendix for other safety thresholds; its values were not supplied, so none are guessed.

The p40 model’s illustrative support first reaches its assumed allowable load at 55% beam power. That is already below the guide’s design requirement: SF falls under 1.5 at about 36.7%. The later cascade near 85% is a visual failure sequence, not a prediction of real hardware failure.

6 · Enablers, energy and forbidden shortcuts

OCTO-EARTH declares exactly three individual enablers—separate assumptions, not bundled systems. The supplied guide allows up to three hypothetical enablers and notes that fewer / smaller assumptions score more honestly. These selections do not establish that supporting equipment is ordinary, feasible, safe or certified.

EnablerSingle selected assumptionWhy selected / modeled scopeBoundary / unresolved gap
E1Access to a finite 3×1022 kg asteroid.Reaction mass is mission-critical. The model tracks a finite reservoir, ejected packets and Earth recoil.Asteroid capture, docking and placement are not simulated or demonstrated. The launch ring, eight arms, power conditioning / reserve and directional radiators are separate supporting systems; launch damage remains unresolved.
E2Thin-film photovoltaic collector field.Five million collectors at 0.5 AU intercept the configured 30% of solar output; panel conversion and the energy ledger are represented.Industrial scale and deployment are unverified. Microwave transmitters, rectennas, sail-tilt station keeping, clear lanes, factories and Mercury feedstock are separately listed supporting equipment / assumptions.
E3Space-mirror ring.Reflection is used to supply sunlight to the simplified climate model at the target orbit; mirrors create no energy.Configured area, real orbits, formation flight, pointing and local illumination are not validated. Orbit / pointing support is separately listed.

Support hardware is itemized separately in the technical brief and calculation report. Calling it “support” is only a classification choice: power levels, material loads, thermal rejection, capture, deployment and safe operation still require independent engineering evidence.

Allowed only when bounded and declared

Examples in the guide include a stated power source and waste heat, energy-density-limited storage, specified high-strength arms, superconducting current density, prebuilt ring/arms, delivered raw material, ideal control/motors, external-field plasma contact and specified asteroid mass. Fewer, smaller enablers are more scientifically honest.

Not allowed

No reactionless thrust; no broken conservation laws; do not switch off gravity, inertia or Earth’s field; do not assume climate stays constant; do not ignore structural loads. The simulation must expose the reaction body and source of every joule.

Energy-bonus credit is not claimed here. The guide awards up to +10 only when a real modeled source supplies the energy within the time limit; source scale, construction, efficiency and waste heat must be shown.
