Environment inventory · what is simulated vs illustrative
  • Physics bodies / states: Sun as a point-mass gravity source; Earth plus remaining docked asteroid mass; the physical Earth–Moon system; released packets/fragments; Mars; and Mercury, Venus and Jupiter. Their recorded positions/velocities drive the graphics; existing fast-model and N-body caveats still apply. Saturn, Uranus and Neptune are added only as schematic display markers.
  • Computed subsystems and support objects: 12 tracked collector-slot/orbit-keeping records, a five-million-collector engineering assumption (about 900 overview marks), an approximately million-mirror design (small rendered samples), solar/beam/launcher energy bookkeeping, two-box global-mean climate, and derived ice/liquid/growing-zone indicators. Fleet and mirrors are not individually simulated gravitating bodies.
  • Illustrative Solar System backdrop: all eight planets are visible, with the simulated inner-body positions plus schematic display-only Saturn/Uranus/Neptune paths. The moon layer shows the physical Earth Moon plus display-only Phobos, Deimos, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Mimas, Enceladus, Tethys, Dione, Rhea, Titan, Iapetus, Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, Oberon and Triton. This curated set is not a complete satellite catalogue; non-Earth moon orbits are decorative. The 2,400 enlarged, irregular, cratered asteroid graphics across 2.1–3.3 AU also follow display-only Kepler paths; they have no catalog identities, gravity, collisions or encounter effects. The target orbit at 2.7 AU overlaps the drawn belt.
  • Surfaces, sizes and Sun: Earth uses bundled NASA-derived day/night/cloud maps; Mercury, Venus, Mars, the Moon and non-Earth planets use procedural crater/band textures. The overview Sun and planet discs are enlarged by about 1.5× for visibility while every recorded position, orbital radius, period and orbit-path geometry stay unchanged; the path lines use brighter display colors. True-scale close-ups remain true-scale. Saturn's ring bands and Uranus's faint ring are schematic. Subtle colour-tinted atmospheric limb shells distinguish Venus, Mars and the giant planets; Saturn's rings use finer separated bands and a Cassini-gap cue. The Sun has faster-moving, lava-like granulation, limb darkening and a restrained corona, plus five small active-region illustrations with ten warm magnetic-loop arcs. The arcs sway, brighten and fade in separate irregular bursts; all are decorative art, not solar observation data or an activity model.
  • Decorative background: 14,000 fine, softly tinted point sprites in the overview; the star shell is inside the camera's far plane so it is not clipped. Close-up views also have denser point stars with gentle fade/twinkle. They are not a star catalogue, do not move under physics and exert no forces.
  • Hardware art: four rigid visual links per arm, exposed hinge drums, rails and three-jaw grippers. Two opposite arms remain braced on the docked asteroid during idle and throws; the other six animate the packet ejection. Collector and arm finishes are less mirror-like/self-lit, while climate mirror plates retain their polished silver environment reflection; photovoltaic covers use a distinct coated-cell sheen. These finishes are renderer-only and do not alter the recorded energy, climate or beam/reflection physics. Hardware strength, collisions, joint dynamics and attachment forces remain outside the simulation.
  • Planet temperatures: click any of the eight planets for a worked black-body estimate using its displayed/modelled Sun distance, NASA Bond albedo, solar flux, global-mean absorbed energy and the Stefan–Boltzmann formula. These simple estimates are clearly separated from NASA mean-temperature references; giant planets have no solid surface, and Saturn, Uranus and Neptune use display-only schematic tracks.
  • Earth climate: clicking Earth or opening Climate shows a draggable split comparison: no space-mirror ring on the left, mirror-ring scenario on the right. The panel reports sunlight, mirror contribution, albedo, the fixed effective greenhouse emissivity, formula and temperatures. Idle values are a labelled 2.7 AU reference; playback uses recorded with/without-mirror climate values. The ring artwork is enlarged; wind/ocean flows are illustrative. Playback continues.
  • Earth-POV environment layers: magnetic dipole, solar wind, Shue magnetopause/bow shock, schematic radiation belts, Hill/gravity-well views and visual beam/mirror rays are simplified display models; they do not feed back into the orbit state.

Quick start

    
    
      
  1. Watch a mission: press ▶ in the bottom bar (or Space). The hand-designed heuristic works without training.
    2. 
      
  2. Speed: drag the slider. Slow = single packets and the Moon; fast = the whole spiral.
    3. 
      
  3. Earth POV: press V or the ◉ View button for the close-up with selectable layers (magnetic field, plasma, gravity, Mars).
    4. 
      
  4. Power beams (button in the bottom bar or key B): ride on an arm and watch the microwave beams from the solar collectors arrive and be absorbed whenever a packet is launched.
    5. 
      
  5. Planet temperatures: click Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus or Neptune for a worked radiative estimate and a separate NASA reference. The giant planets have no solid surface; Saturn, Uranus and Neptune are display-only here.
    6. 
      
  6. Climate (bottom-right button or key W): Earth opens a large mirror-ring before/after view. Drag the vertical divider: left is without the ring, right is the mirror-ring scenario. The calculation card shows sunlight, mirror input, albedo, greenhouse/emissivity assumptions, formula and result; at 2.7 AU the no-mirror Earth is a frozen snowball. Playback continues; click ← Overview to return.
    7. 
      
  7. Collector (key C): a close-up of one of the 5 million solar collector satellites – solar wings facing the Sun, microwave transmitter beaming to Earth's arms.
    8. 
      
  8. Mars (red) is fully simulated: it pulls Earth and is pulled back. Earth must stay outside the safe distance (red ring) – the safety shield steers around it.
    9. 
      
  9. Train / compare in ▶ Mission, results in ▤ Results, every explanation in ? Guide. Hover anything with ⓘ.
